# Implemented scope and remaining limits

- The local backend remains a single-process Colyseus service with SQLite WAL. The optional Supabase adapter authenticates human accounts, stores private gathering images, and retries hosted record mirrors. SQLite on a persistent volume remains authoritative. Cleared missions and results survive restarts; live rooms do not.
- Fifteen mission definitions and seven condensed demo missions are implemented. The 45–60 minute normal and 8–12 minute demo labels are first-play pacing targets, not measured guarantees. Fast automated pilots complete some mechanics much sooner. Normal difficulty, onboarding/read time, and first-time campaign duration still need human playtesting.
- The renderer is illustrated 2.5D: isometric projection, upright depth-sorted rigs, articulated warrior limbs and horse gait, limited mirrored facings, scenery, and procedural geometry. It is not a full 3D camera or complete directional animation set. Monsters use full-body illustrations and attack effects. Missing images show Phaser placeholders; there is no runtime image generation. Software WebGL was slow on the test host; automatic Canvas fallback improved its measured frame rate. Other hardware/browser performance still requires deployment testing.
- Normal duels use sword/shield equipment. Story riding and wind-affected thrown spears are implemented; squad roles provide spear reach, ranged projectiles, protection and mobility. Separate mounted/ranged duel queues, additional competitive disciplines, face customization, and mobile combat controls remain deferred.
- Campaign progress, honor, first-clear rewards and cosmetic ranks are separate from competitive duel statistics. Legacy records and stage unlocks remain; they do not auto-complete new missions. Replaying a mission does not duplicate first-clear points/honor. Narrative pacing and normal combat difficulty remain tuning areas.
- Local identity is a signed guest session. Clearing browser storage loses access to that guest account. Supabase email authentication is required for genuine gathering verification. Wallet possession verification remains optional; thirdweb onboarding is unavailable.
- Genuine gathering requires four qualifying authenticated accounts, a consented private image and all four approvals. This proves account approvals, not biometric identity or physical co-location. Demo gathering is explicitly simulated. The Royal Lunch Pass is a prototype without real-world redemption or commercial partnership.
- Achievements remain off-chain inventory. Attestation and ERC-1155 authorization/replay tests run locally; automated inventory minting and sponsored transactions are not implemented. Base Sepolia broadcasts require authorized deployment, funded keys and configuration. Demo chain submissions remain simulated.
- Sound is synthesized locally with gesture-based unlocking, mute and volume persistence. A recorded music soundtrack and spatial audio are deferred. Browser audio restrictions still apply.
- Physics supports projection-safe aiming, movement prediction/reconciliation, interpolation, collision, mounted acceleration/braking/turning, swept projectiles, stamina and knockback. It does not implement rollback hit validation, full navigation meshes, lag-compensated attacks, production anti-cheat or multi-instance room recovery.
- Menus support narrow screens. Keyboard/mouse combat is desktop-first. Reduced motion disables scroll/ember animation and camera shake; articulated movement remains visible for readability.
- Match history stores significant authoritative events rather than deterministic replay/video. The leaderboard shows individual competitive ratings; separate squad/campaign/crown leaderboard views remain deferred.
- Railway, the existing Supabase upgrade, genuine four-account upload/approval, and production WSS must be verified after the owner deploys/configures the backend. No Railway backend URL was available during this implementation. The code and instructions do not constitute a public deployment.
- Ganache uses its JavaScript WebSocket fallback on this Node 22 environment. The optional native µWS warning does not affect contract assertions. OpenZeppelin remains pinned to 5.3.0 with the tested lockfile.
